What you are seeing

Three effects (evaporator bodies) connected in series. Steam enters Effect 1 (left, hottest). Purified water boils and produces vapour which passes to Effect 2 as heating medium. Effect 2 vapour heats Effect 3. Each effect operates at progressively lower pressure and temperature. Final vapour from Effect 3 condenses as pure sterile WFI.

Key Point: 1 kg of steam in a 7-effect still produces approximately 6.5 kg of WFI — far more energy-efficient than a single-effect still. WFI specification (IP/USP): conductivity less than or equal to 1.3 microS/cm at 25 degrees C, endotoxins less than 0.25 EU/mL, sterile and pyrogen-free.

Q1. WFI (Water for Injection) must be produced by distillation (or membrane technology in USP 2017+) rather than purification alone because:
Correct. Endotoxin removal is the critical requirement: bacterial endotoxins (LPS, MW 10,000-100,000 Da) are non-volatile — cannot vaporise even at 100 degrees C. They are left behind in the still pot while pure water vapour passes to the condenser. This is why distillation is definitive for WFI. Modern alternative (USP General Chapter 1231 since 2017): membrane purification (nanofiltration + ultrafiltration + UV treatment + electrodeionisation) can also produce WFI-grade water. However IP (Indian Pharmacopoeia) still requires distillation as the only acceptable method for WFI.
Q2. In a triple-effect WFI still, Effect 2 is heated by:
Correct. Multiple effect principle: vapour from Effect 1 (at T1, P1) is used as heating medium for Effect 2 (at T2 < T1, P2 < P1). Effect 2 operates at lower pressure so its boiling point is lower — the vapour from Effect 1 can condense and give up its latent heat to boil water in Effect 2. This steam economy (using vapour as heating medium) repeats: Effect 2 vapour heats Effect 3. Result: one unit of primary steam produces approximately N units of WFI in an N-effect still. For 7-effect still: 1 kg steam produces approximately 6.5 kg WFI — 6.5x more efficient than single effect.
