💡 What you are seeing

Hot saturated solution (red) enters from the LEFT and flows along the U-shaped trough. Cooling water flows through the jacket beneath the trough — temperature drops from left to right. As the solution cools, supersaturation is reached and crystals (white squares) nucleate and grow. The slowly rotating helical screw moves the crystal slurry toward the RIGHT discharge end. Temperature gradient shown above trough.

🔑 The Swenson-Walker is a cooling crystalliser. Solubility of most APIs decreases as temperature falls — hot saturated solution becomes supersaturated on cooling → crystals form. The screw prevents settling and cake formation.

Construction and Working

  • Trough: Open U-shaped SS trough, 600 mm wide, up to 12 m long
  • Cooling jacket: Cold water flows beneath and around trough walls
  • Screw conveyor: Helical screw (450 mm dia), 0.5–5 rpm — moves crystals and prevents settling
  • Feed: Hot saturated solution enters one end; crystal slurry exits other end
  • Multiple units: Can be arranged in series (cascade) at progressively lower temperatures
🔑 Pharmaceutical uses: Aspirin (from ethyl acetate), citric acid, tartaric acid, vitamin C, sodium chloride, riboflavin — all crystallised in Swenson-Walker units.
Q1. Supersaturation in the Swenson-Walker Crystalliser is achieved by:
✅ Swenson-Walker = cooling crystalliser. Most pharmaceutical compounds have higher solubility at higher temperatures. Cooling → solubility drops → existing concentration exceeds new solubility → supersaturation → nucleation → crystal growth.
Q2. The purpose of the rotating screw in the Swenson-Walker Crystalliser is:
✅ The screw rotates slowly (0.5–5 rpm) — fast enough to keep crystals suspended and moving forward, slow enough to avoid crystal breakage. Without the screw, crystals would settle and form a hard immovable cake at the bottom of the trough.
