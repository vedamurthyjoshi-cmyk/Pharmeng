🔑 The Swenson-Walker Crystalliser cools a saturated solution by indirect heat exchange through a jacketed trough. Cooling creates supersaturation → crystal nucleation and growth. The rotating screw provides gentle agitation and moves crystals toward the discharge end.

Principle

Crystallisation occurs when a solution becomes supersaturated — the concentration of solute exceeds its equilibrium solubility at that temperature. The Swenson-Walker crystalliser achieves supersaturation by cooling: hot saturated solution enters → cooling jacket removes heat → solubility decreases → solution becomes supersaturated → crystals nucleate and grow.

Construction

  • Trough: Open, U-shaped stainless steel trough; 600 mm wide, up to 12 m long; may be arranged in sections
  • Cooling jacket: Water-cooled jacket below and around trough sides; cooling water flows in counter-current to solution flow
  • Screw conveyor: Single helical screw (450 mm diameter); rotates slowly (0.5–5 rpm); moves crystals from feed end to discharge end while keeping them suspended
  • Drive: Motor and gearbox drive the screw; low speed to prevent crystal breakage
  • Discharge: Crystals + mother liquor exit at the discharge end; separated by centrifuge or filter
🔑 Multiple units can be connected in series (each at a lower temperature) to achieve progressive crystallisation and recover more product. This is called a cascade or train arrangement.

Pharmaceutical Applications

  • Crystallisation of aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) from ethyl acetate or acetic acid solution
  • Crystallisation of citric acid, tartaric acid, sodium chloride
  • Crystallisation of vitamins (ascorbic acid, riboflavin) from aqueous solution
  • Manufacture of bulk pharmaceutical chemicals (BPCs) requiring large crystal size

Advantages and Disadvantages

  • ✅ Continuous operation — high throughput
  • ✅ Gentle agitation — large, uniform crystals produced
  • ✅ Simple construction — easy to maintain
  • ✅ Easy scale-up by adding sections
  • ❌ Open trough — contamination risk; not suitable for sterile products
  • ❌ Limited to cooling crystallisation — not for salting-out or anti-solvent
  • ❌ Crystal breakage possible if screw speed too high
Q1. In the Swenson-Walker Crystalliser, supersaturation is achieved by:
✅ Swenson-Walker = cooling crystalliser. Solubility of most pharmaceutical compounds increases with temperature. Hot saturated solution enters → cooling jacket removes heat → temperature drops → solubility decreases → concentration exceeds new solubility → supersaturation → nucleation and crystal growth. The screw prevents settling and moves crystals to discharge. Compare Oslo crystalliser (circulation crystalliser — allows larger crystals to grow).
Q2. The rotating screw in a Swenson-Walker Crystalliser serves which purpose?
✅ Screw functions: (1) Conveyance — moves crystal slurry from hot feed end toward cooler discharge end (residence time = 30–90 min); (2) Agitation — gentle rotation keeps crystals suspended, prevents settling into a solid cake, prevents crystal-to-crystal agglomeration; (3) Heat transfer improvement — gentle agitation improves contact between solution and cooled trough surface. Speed is kept low (0.5–5 rpm) to prevent crystal breakage — excessive shear creates fines (small crystals) that are harder to filter.
Q3. Aspirin is crystallised from ethyl acetate solution using a Swenson-Walker crystalliser. The yield of crystals depends primarily on:
✅ Yield calculation: theoretical yield = mass of solute that crystallises = feed concentration (g/L) − equilibrium solubility at outlet T (g/L), multiplied by total volume processed. Example: aspirin solubility in EtOAc at 50°C = 300 g/L; at 10°C = 50 g/L. If feed is saturated at 50°C → theoretical yield = 300 − 50 = 250 g per litre of solution processed = 83% yield. Higher ΔT (lower outlet T) = higher yield. But too fast cooling = many small crystals (poor filtration) vs slow cooling = large crystals (easy filtration).
