What you are seeing

Three evaporator bodies (effects) are connected in series. Steam enters Effect 1 at the highest pressure and temperature. Vapour produced in Effect 1 is used as heating steam for Effect 2 (at lower pressure and temperature). Vapour from Effect 2 heats Effect 3. Feed enters Effect 1 and becomes progressively more concentrated through each effect. Final vapour from Effect 3 goes to the condenser.

Key Point: Energy economy -- in a single effect, 1 kg steam evaporates approximately 1 kg water. In a triple effect: 1 kg steam evaporates approximately 2.7 kg water. In a 7-effect WFI still: 1 kg steam produces approximately 6.5 kg WFI. Multiple effect dramatically reduces operating cost.

Q1. In the Multiple Effect Evaporator, successive effects operate at progressively lower pressure because:
Correct. Temperature cascade: Effect 1 at 1.2 bar, boiling at 105C. Effect 2 at 0.5 bar, boiling at 80C. Effect 3 at 0.15 bar, boiling at 55C. Vapour from Effect 1 at 105C can heat Effect 2 liquid at 80C (25C driving force). Vapour from Effect 2 at 80C can heat Effect 3 at 55C (25C driving force). Each kg of input steam does useful work in all 3 effects.
Q2. The 7-effect falling film WFI still achieves high energy efficiency because:
Correct. Economy ratio of N-effect evaporator = approximately (N-0.5) kg water per kg steam. 7-effect: economy = 6.5. Pharmaceutical benefit: WFI production is energy-intensive and continuous. A 7-effect still versus a single-effect still reduces steam consumption by 85% -- significant cost saving at pharmaceutical scale (typically 1,000-10,000 L/hr WFI). Capital cost of extra vessels is quickly recovered in energy savings.
