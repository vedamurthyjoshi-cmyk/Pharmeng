💡 What you are seeing

Slurry (blue particles) is pumped under pressure from the left. It enters chambers between filter plates. Liquid passes through the filter cloth (yellow lines) and exits as clear filtrate (green arrows). Solid particles are retained and build up as a filter cake (brown layer) between the plates. When cake is thick enough, the press is opened and cake discharged.

🔑 Filter Press = pressure filtration. The pump provides 2–8 bar pressure to push liquid through the filter cloth. Higher pressure = faster filtration rate but thicker, drier cake. No moving parts during filtration — reliable and simple.

Principle

The Filter Press forces slurry through a filter medium (cloth) under pressure (2–8 bar from a pump). Liquid (filtrate) passes through the cloth and is collected; solids are retained on the cloth surface, building up as a filter cake. As cake thickness increases, filtration resistance rises — when resistance becomes too high, filtration is stopped, plates opened, and cake discharged.

Construction

  • Frame: Horizontal steel frame with end plates — holds plates under compression
  • Filter plates: Recessed or flush plates; grooved surfaces support filter cloth and allow filtrate drainage
  • Filter cloth: Woven fabric (cotton, polypropylene, nylon) stretched over each plate; acts as filter medium
  • Feed inlet: Slurry pumped through central port in one end plate
  • Filtrate outlets: Each plate has drain cock — clear filtrate exits individually or through common manifold
  • Hydraulic closer: Tightens all plates together under pressure before filtration begins

Pharmaceutical Applications

  • Isolation of API after crystallisation from mother liquor
  • Removal of activated charcoal (decolourisation) from pharmaceutical solutions
  • Clarification of large volumes of pharmaceutical intermediates
  • Separation of catalyst from reaction mixture in API synthesis
🔑 Filter Press produces the driest cake of common batch filters — because high pressure squeezes residual liquid from cake pores. Useful when API must be as dry as possible before final drying step.
Q1. The driving force for filtration in a Filter Press is:
✅ Darcy's Law governs filter press: Q = (ΔP × A) / (μ × R_total) where Q = filtration rate, ΔP = pressure drop, A = filter area, μ = viscosity, R_total = resistance of cloth + cake. Higher ΔP (pump pressure) → higher Q (faster filtration). As cake builds: R_cake increases → Q decreases even at constant pressure. Filter press typically operates at constant pressure (pump) — filtration rate declines over the batch as cake thickens.
Q2. The filter cake in a Filter Press is washed before discharge to:
✅ Cake washing in filter press: after filtration, cake pores contain mother liquor rich in impurities, by-products, excess reagents. Wash procedure: close cake in press → pump wash solvent (water, methanol) through cake → wash liquor displaces mother liquor → collected separately (as wash fraction). Number of wash volumes = wash ratio. Higher wash ratio = purer cake = better API quality (fewer impurities). Wash liquor may be recycled for solvent recovery. Essential step in pharmaceutical API isolation to meet purity specification.
Q3. A filter press is classified as which type of filter?
✅ Batch nature of filter press: as cake builds, resistance increases → at some point, filtration rate becomes too slow to be economical → must stop. Open press → pull plates apart → cake discharged by scraping or dropping → press reassembled → new filtration cycle. Typical batch time: 2–8 hours depending on slurry concentration and target cake dryness. For continuous filtration: use Rotary Drum Filter instead — all stages happen simultaneously as drum rotates.
