What you are seeing

Liquid flows from the wide pipe into the converging (tapering) section, then through the narrow THROAT, then diverges back to the original pipe diameter. As liquid enters the throat, velocity INCREASES (continuity: Av = constant). By Bernoulli, increased velocity means decreased pressure. The pressure difference (P1 - P2) measured between the inlet and throat is directly related to flow rate.

Key Point: Flow equation -- Q = Cd x A_throat x sqrt(2 x deltaP / (rho x (1 - (A_throat/A_pipe)^2))). Discharge coefficient Cd approximately 0.98. Low permanent pressure loss (only 10-20% of deltaP lost permanently) -- most pressure recovered in the diverging section.

Venturimeter -- Theory

  • Converging section: 20-30 degree angle; smooth contraction to throat
  • Throat: Minimum diameter (50-75% of pipe diameter); pressure tap here (P2)
  • Diverging section: 5-7 degree angle; slow expansion recovers pressure (avoids separation)
  • Pressure measurement: Differential pressure transmitter between upstream tap (P1) and throat tap (P2)
  • Pharmaceutical use: WFI flow in distribution loops; CIP solution flow; process fluid flow monitoring

Q1. The pressure at the Venturimeter throat is lower than at the inlet because:
Correct. Bernoulli application: P1 + 0.5*rho*v1^2 = P2 + 0.5*rho*v2^2. Rearranging: P1 - P2 = 0.5*rho*(v2^2 - v1^2). Since v2 > v1 (throat velocity greater than inlet velocity), P2 < P1. The pressure drop deltaP = P1 - P2 is proportional to v2^2, and therefore to Q^2. So Q proportional to sqrt(deltaP) -- that is why the venturimeter equation contains a square root.
Q2. The Venturimeter has lower permanent pressure loss than the Orifice Meter because:
Correct. Pressure recovery: Venturimeter -- smooth diffuser converts velocity back to pressure efficiently -- permanent loss only 10-20% of measured deltaP. Orifice meter -- sudden expansion downstream of orifice -- turbulent eddies dissipate kinetic energy as heat -- permanent loss 60% of deltaP. For WFI systems where pressure is limited (to avoid pump cavitation and maintain flow to all use points), Venturimeter is preferred despite higher cost.
