Solvent Recovery in Pharmaceutical Industry

Solvents are the largest waste stream in pharmaceutical manufacturing. Recovery reduces costs, environmental impact, and complies with solvent emission regulations.

Process Overview

Spent solvent mixture enters the recovery system. Distillation separates solvents based on boiling points. Recovered pure solvent is reused in manufacturing. Residue (bottoms) is disposed as hazardous waste.

Distillation Column

The heart of solvent recovery. The column has trays or packing. Vapour rises, liquid descends. Lighter (lower BP) solvent enriches at top; heavier solvent at bottom. A rectification section above the feed and stripping section below.

Condenser

Overhead vapours are condensed using cooling water. Distillate is collected as recovered pure solvent. Some is returned as reflux to improve separation (reflux ratio 1:3–1:5).

Reboiler

Steam-heated reboiler at the column base provides vapour for separation. Controls residue removal rate.

Common Solvents Recovered

Ethanol, IPA, acetone, methanol, ethyl acetate, dichloromethane, toluene. Purity typically 99–99.9% after recovery.
