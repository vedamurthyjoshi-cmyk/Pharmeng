Cold Chain Management — Temperature-Controlled Supply

Pharmaceutical Cold Chain

The cold chain is an unbroken temperature-controlled supply chain that ensures temperature-sensitive pharmaceutical products (vaccines, biologics, insulin) remain within specified conditions from manufacture to patient.

Temperature Categories

  • Room temperature: 15–25°C — most tablets, capsules
  • Refrigerated (CRT): +2 to +8°C — vaccines, insulin, many biologics
  • Frozen: −15 to −25°C — some vaccines (varicella, MMR)
  • Ultra-cold: −60 to −80°C — mRNA vaccines (e.g. Pfizer-BioNTech COVID-19)
  • Cryogenic: −150°C and below — cell/gene therapies in liquid nitrogen

Cold Chain Elements

  • Cold store warehouse → refrigerated transport vehicle → distribution hub
  • Qualified thermal packaging (insulated shippers, phase-change materials)
  • Continuous temperature data loggers and real-time monitoring
  • Mean Kinetic Temperature (MKT) calculations for excursion assessment
  • ICH Q1A and WHO guidelines for temperature excursion management
