🔥 Short-Tube (Calandria) Evaporator

Pharmaceutical Engineering BP304T · Chapter 8 — Evaporation

💡 What you are seeing

Steam surrounds the short vertical tubes (calandria). Liquid inside the tubes is heated and rises upward (hot liquid is less dense). It enters the vapour space above, where vapour bubbles escape upward (white dots). Cooler, denser liquid descends through the central downcomer (large central pipe) back to the bottom — creating a natural circulation loop. Concentrated liquid exits at the bottom.

🔑 Natural circulation — no pump needed. The density difference between hot liquid in tubes and cooler liquid in the downcomer drives the circulation. Simple, robust, widely used for pharmaceutical evaporation where the product is not heat-sensitive.

Principle

The Calandria (short-tube) evaporator achieves evaporation by natural circulation. Steam condenses on the outside of short vertical tubes (1–2 m long, 50–100 mm diameter). Liquid inside the tubes heats up, density decreases, liquid rises. In the vapour space above the tubes, vapour separates from liquid. Cooler, denser liquid flows down the central downcomer back to the bottom. This creates a continuous natural circulation without any pump.

Construction

  • Calandria (tube bundle): Short vertical tubes (1–2 m) fixed between upper and lower tube sheets; steam in the shell side
  • Central downcomer: Large-diameter central pipe (200–500 mm); liquid descends through this
  • Vapour space: Large open space above tube bundle for vapour-liquid separation
  • Steam chest: Shell surrounding the tubes; steam inlet and condensate outlet
  • Feed inlet: Dilute solution enters at bottom; concentrated product exits at bottom

Pharmaceutical Applications and Limitations

  • Concentration of inorganic salt solutions (NaCl, KCl, ammonium sulphate) in pharmaceutical synthesis
  • Concentration of sugar syrups and liquid oral formulations
  • NOT suitable for heat-sensitive products — long residence time (minutes to hours) in hot liquid
  • NOT suitable for viscous liquids (poor natural circulation) or scaling/crystallising products
🔑 The short-tube evaporator is the OLDEST evaporator design — simple, reliable, and easy to maintain. Its main limitation is long liquid residence time making it unsuitable for heat-sensitive pharmaceutical products like vitamins, antibiotics, or proteins.
Q1. In a Short-Tube Calandria Evaporator, liquid circulation is driven by:
✅ Natural circulation principle: density of liquid decreases as temperature increases. Hot liquid in steam-heated tubes (less dense) rises due to buoyancy. At top, vapour separates. Cooler concentrated liquid (denser) descends through central downcomer. This density-driven loop circulates liquid continuously without any external pump — simplifying construction and maintenance. Circulation rate depends on tube length, temperature difference, and liquid viscosity.
Q2. The Short-Tube Evaporator is NOT recommended for concentration of antibiotic solutions because:
✅ Residence time comparison: Short-tube/Calandria = liquid recirculates continuously → effective residence time = minutes to hours. Falling Film = liquid passes once through tube in 5–30 seconds. For heat-sensitive APIs (antibiotics like penicillin, erythromycin; vitamins; enzymes): use Falling Film under vacuum (lower boiling temperature + short time = minimal degradation). Short-tube is acceptable for thermally stable molecules: inorganic salts, sugars, non-heat-sensitive APIs.
Q3. The purpose of the central downcomer in a Calandria Evaporator is:
✅ Downcomer function: without the central downcomer, liquid would have no return path → circulation would stop → tubes would run dry → fouling and damage. The downcomer (typically 25–40% of total cross-sectional area) provides low-resistance return flow path. Large diameter = low velocity = low pressure drop = good natural circulation. If downcomer is too small → high velocity → high pressure drop → reduced circulation → poor evaporation efficiency.
