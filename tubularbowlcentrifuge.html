What you are seeing

The long narrow bowl (bowl diameter ~100mm, length ~750mm) rotates at 15,000-50,000 rpm. Feed enters at the BOTTOM through a stationary nozzle. Centrifugal force (up to 62,000xg) throws dense particles and heavy liquid phase to the bowl WALL. Light phase (clarified liquid) rises and overflows at the TOP. Solids accumulate on the wall and must be removed manually by stopping the bowl.

Key Point: The Tubular Bowl generates the highest centrifugal force of any pharmaceutical centrifuge -- up to 62,000xg. This separates particles smaller than 1 micrometre that cannot be filtered by any conventional filter.

Tubular Bowl Centrifuge -- Construction and Working

  • Bowl: Narrow cylindrical tube (D=100mm, L=750mm), high-tensile steel or SS 316L
  • Speed: 15,000-50,000 rpm generating 13,000-62,000xg
  • Feed: Enters at bottom via stationary nozzle; liquid rises through bowl
  • Separation: Dense phase (heavy liquid or solids) accumulates at wall; light phase overflows at top
  • Solids discharge: Manual -- bowl must be stopped, disassembled, scraped -- batch operation
  • Pharmaceutical use: Clarification of blood plasma, enzyme solutions, viral vaccines, colloidal preparations
Key Fact: The Tubular Bowl has NO continuous solids discharge -- it must be stopped and manually cleaned when solid capacity is reached. This limits throughput for heavily-loaded feeds. Use Disc Centrifuge for continuous operation.
Q1. The Tubular Bowl Centrifuge is used for pharmaceutical clarification because:
Correct. At 50,000rpm with 50mm radius: g-force = 34,600xg. This force sediments virus particles (50-200nm), colloidal particles, and fine precipitates that no filter can retain. Applications: plasma protein fractionation; virus concentration for vaccines; bacteria removal from fermentation broth.
Q2. Solids discharge from the Tubular Bowl Centrifuge requires:
Correct. Manual discharge makes the Tubular Bowl a batch centrifuge despite continuous liquid flow. When bowl fills with solids (typically every 30-120 minutes), the entire machine must stop, be opened in a clean area, scraped, cleaned, reassembled, and restarted -- labour intensive and a contamination risk.
