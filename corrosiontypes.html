Types of Corrosion in Pharmaceutical Equipment

Corrosion is the degradation of metals by chemical or electrochemical reaction with the environment. In pharmaceutical manufacturing, corrosion can contaminate products and damage equipment.

1. Uniform / General Corrosion

Metal surface corrodes uniformly across the whole area. Predictable and manageable. Common in mild steel exposed to acids.

2. Pitting Corrosion

Localised attack forming deep pits while the rest of the surface remains intact. Dangerous because pits are hard to detect. Common in stainless steel exposed to chlorides.

3. Crevice Corrosion

Occurs in confined spaces (gaskets, flanges, under deposits) where oxygen is depleted. Creates differential aeration cells.

4. Galvanic Corrosion

When two dissimilar metals are in contact in an electrolyte. The less noble metal (anode) corrodes preferentially. Prevented by avoiding dissimilar metal contacts.

5. Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC)

Combination of tensile stress and corrosive environment causes cracking. Can be sudden and catastrophic. Common in austenitic stainless steel in chloride environments.

Prevention

Use SS316L for pharmaceutical contact parts. Apply passivation (citric/nitric acid treatment). Regular inspection and electropolishing of surfaces.
