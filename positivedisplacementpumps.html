What you are seeing

LEFT: Peristaltic pump -- rotating rollers compress a flexible tube sequentially, squeezing liquid forward in a wave. Only the tubing contacts the product. RIGHT: Gear pump -- two interlocking gears trap liquid in the spaces between teeth and carry it from inlet to outlet. Fixed volume discharged per revolution regardless of pressure.

Key Point: Positive displacement pumps deliver a FIXED volume per revolution -- flow rate is proportional to speed, independent of back pressure. This makes them ideal for accurate metering. Compare centrifugal pump: flow decreases as pressure increases.

Positive Displacement Pumps -- Theory

  • Peristaltic: Rollers compress tubing; only tubing contacts product; zero contamination; sterile (pre-sterilised tubing); accurate; used for bioprocessing, aseptic filling, chromatography
  • Gear pump: Two gears mesh; liquid carried in tooth spaces; high pressure capability; used for viscous oils, ointments, suppository mass
  • Diaphragm pump: Reciprocating diaphragm; no shaft seal; used for aggressive solvents and acids
  • All PD pumps: NEVER close discharge valve while running -- pressure builds to infinity -- pump or pipe failure
Key Point: Positive displacement pumps deliver a FIXED volume per revolution -- flow rate is proportional to speed, independent of back pressure. This makes them ideal for accurate metering. Compare centrifugal pump: flow decreases as pressure increases.
Q1. The peristaltic pump is preferred for pharmaceutical sterile filling because:
Correct. Sterile filling peristaltic pump advantages: (1) Disposable tubing -- no cross-contamination between batches; (2) No shaft seal -- seal failure cannot contaminate product; (3) Pre-sterilised tubing eliminates sterilisation validation; (4) Accurate metering (plus/minus 0.5% per revolution) -- essential for filling accuracy; (5) Can pump abrasive API suspensions -- tubing is the only wearing part and is disposable.
Q2. A gear pump is suitable for pumping pharmaceutical ointment (viscosity 10,000 mPa.s) but a centrifugal pump is not because:
Correct. Centrifugal pump limitation for viscous fluids: pump head H proportional to v^2/2g, and for viscous fluid, the velocity imparted by impeller is dramatically reduced by viscous drag -- net head decreases to near zero. Gear pump for ointments: output pressure up to 50 bar; flow independent of viscosity; shear rate between gear teeth can be controlled to avoid product degradation. Typical pharmaceutical gear pump applications: pumping ointment from manufacturing vessel to filling line; transferring suppository mass; handling thick creams.
