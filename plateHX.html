What you are seeing

Corrugated metal plates are clamped together. Hot fluid (red) and cold fluid (blue) flow in ALTERNATE channels between adjacent plates -- they never mix but are separated only by the thin plate wall. The corrugated (chevron) pattern promotes turbulence, dramatically increasing the heat transfer coefficient. Plates can be added to increase capacity.

Key Point: Plate HX achieves 3-5 times higher heat transfer coefficient than shell-and-tube because the corrugated surface forces turbulent flow even at low velocities -- very thin boundary layer -- rapid heat transfer across the thin plate (typically 0.6mm SS).

Q1. The corrugated (chevron) pattern on Plate HX plates improves heat transfer because:
Correct. In laminar flow (smooth channels), heat must conduct through a thick stagnant boundary layer -- very slow transfer. Corrugated channels: fluid constantly redirected by chevron ridges -- generates turbulence (Re > 400 even at low velocity) -- thin boundary layer -- high h. PHE overall U-value: 3,000-6,000 W/m2K vs Shell-and-Tube: 500-2,000 W/m2K.
Q2. The Plate Heat Exchanger is preferred for pharmaceutical CIP (Clean-in-Place) because:
Correct. GMP advantage of disassembly: visual inspection of every plate surface for scratches, pits, or biofilm -- impossible in shell-and-tube. Pharmaceutical crossover cleaning verification: if Product A then Product B, must confirm no A residue in HX -- plate disassembly and swab testing provides definitive proof. Many pharmaceutical plants use PHE for this reason despite higher cost vs shell-and-tube.
