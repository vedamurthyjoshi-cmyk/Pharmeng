What you are seeing

A centrifugal pump (bottom right) forces liquid through the heat exchanger tubes at HIGH velocity (2-6 m/s). The liquid is heated but does NOT boil inside the tubes -- system pressure is maintained above boiling point. When liquid enters the vapour body (large vessel, top left) through the flash valve, pressure drops suddenly -- the liquid FLASHES to vapour. Concentrated liquid settles out and recirculates.

Key Point: The Forced Circulation Evaporator prevents boiling inside the heat exchanger tubes by maintaining elevated pressure. This prevents crystal deposition on tube walls -- making it the ONLY evaporator suitable for crystallising solutions such as sodium chloride, ammonium sulphate, and sugar.

Q1. The Forced Circulation Evaporator is used for crystallising solutions because:
Correct. In other evaporators (falling film, short-tube), liquid boils on hot tube surfaces -- if the product is near saturation, crystals nucleate on the hot wall (scale) -- tubes block -- catastrophic fouling. Forced circulation: high velocity turbulence prevents stagnant zones; elevated pressure prevents boiling at the wall; crystals only form in the flash zone where controlled supersaturation occurs.
Q2. Flash evaporation in the Forced Circulation Evaporator occurs when:
Correct. Flash evaporation thermodynamics: if liquid is heated to T at pressure P1 (above boiling point at P1) then enters vapour body at pressure P2 (below boiling point at P2), the liquid is now superheated relative to P2 -- it instantly evaporates until temperature drops to boiling point at P2. The fraction evaporated = Cp*(T1-T2)/lambda where lambda = latent heat. This is efficient, gentle, and controllable.
