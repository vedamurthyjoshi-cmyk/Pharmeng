🌬️ Fluid Bed Dryer — Fluidised Bed Drying

Pharmaceutical Engineering BP304T · Chapter 7 — Drying

🏠 Home

💡 What you are seeing

Hot air (50–80°C) enters from the bottom through a perforated distributor plate, lifting the granules and keeping them suspended — fluidised. Each particle is individually surrounded by hot air from all sides — very efficient drying. Granules change colour from blue (wet) to orange (dry). Moist exhaust air exits at the top through bag filters.

🔑 FBD dries 5–10× faster than a Tray Dryer because each granule is individually surrounded by hot air (360° exposure). Typical drying time: 20–40 minutes vs 8–24 hours for tray dryer.

Principle

Hot air is blown upward through a perforated distributor plate at a velocity exceeding the minimum fluidisation velocity (Umf) for the granules. The granules are lifted off the plate and suspended in the air stream — creating a fluidised bed. In this state, each granule behaves like a fluid particle — moving freely and being surrounded by hot air on all surfaces. Heat and mass transfer are extremely efficient.

Construction

  • Product bowl: Stainless steel bowl containing granules; sits on the main body; easily removable for loading/unloading
  • Distributor plate: Perforated SS plate at bowl base; distributes air evenly across bowl cross-section
  • Expansion chamber: Wider upper section — air velocity drops → granules disengage from air → fall back into fluidised bed; prevents granule entrainment
  • Bag filters: Fabric bags in upper chamber; trap fine particles carried upward by air; shaken periodically to return fines to bed
  • Inlet air heater: Steam coils or electric heater; heats air to 50–80°C
  • Exhaust fan: Pulls air through the system; controls air velocity and fluidisation

Drying Endpoint

  • Outlet temperature method: Outlet air temperature stays constant (wet-bulb T) during drying; rises sharply when drying complete (no more evaporative cooling)
  • LOD sampling: Samples taken every 15–30 min; dried to constant weight; LOD% calculated; drying stopped when LOD meets specification (e.g., NMT 2.0%)
  • NIR spectroscopy (PAT): In-line near-infrared probe measures moisture continuously — real-time endpoint detection
🔑 FBD is also used for fluid bed granulation (Wurster process) — binder solution sprayed into fluidised powder bed → granules form in situ. Single equipment for granulation AND drying — reduces processing time and cross-contamination risk.

Advantages and Disadvantages

  • ✅ Very fast drying (20–40 min) — much faster than tray dryer
  • ✅ Uniform drying — each particle individually exposed — no case hardening
  • ✅ Easy scale-up from lab to production
  • ✅ Can combine granulation and drying (one-pot processing)
  • ❌ Not suitable for sticky, cohesive, or very dense materials (cannot fluidise)
  • ❌ Attrition of fragile granules due to particle-particle collisions
  • ❌ Segregation risk for multicomponent systems of different densities
  • ❌ Explosion risk with organic solvents (special explosion-proof design needed)
Q1. The Fluid Bed Dryer dries granules faster than the Tray Dryer because:
✅ Heat and mass transfer in FBD: Nu = h·d/k and Sh = k_m·d/D (Nusselt and Sherwood numbers). In fluidised bed, each particle surrounded by fresh hot air → thin boundary layer → high heat transfer coefficient h → rapid moisture evaporation. Tray dryer: static bed — air contacts only top surface layer → slow drying. FBD also avoids case hardening (surface over-drying) because uniform drying throughout particle. FBD heat transfer coefficient: 200–400 W/m²·K vs tray dryer: 20–40 W/m²·K — 10× difference.
Q2. The outlet temperature in a Fluid Bed Dryer rises sharply during drying. This indicates:
✅ Outlet temperature drying endpoint: while moisture is evaporating → evaporative cooling keeps granule and outlet air temperature near wet-bulb temperature (typically 30–45°C for 60°C inlet air). When granules are dry → no evaporation → no cooling → outlet temperature rises toward inlet temperature (may reach 55–60°C). GMP practice: set outlet temperature alarm at endpoint temperature (e.g., 45°C) → alarm triggers LOD test → confirms drying complete. This is cheaper and simpler than real-time NIR but equally reliable for most granulations.
Q3. A cohesive, sticky granulation cannot be dried in a Fluid Bed Dryer because:
✅ Fluidisation requirement: for fluidisation, drag force of upward air on particle > particle weight. Umf (minimum fluidisation velocity) depends on particle size, density, and shape. Cohesive particles: inter-particle attraction > air drag → particles stick together → form channels in bed (channelling) or large clumps → uneven drying or complete failure to fluidise. Alternative for cohesive materials: (1) Tray dryer (static bed — no fluidisation needed); (2) Microwave dryer; (3) Drum dryer for very wet/sticky materials.
