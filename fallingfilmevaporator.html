💡 What you are seeing

Feed liquid enters at the TOP and flows DOWN the inside of vertical tubes as a thin film (gravity-driven). Steam condenses on the outside of the tubes, heating the thin film. The short contact time and thin film mean very efficient evaporation with minimal heat damage. Vapour rises up through the tube centre while concentrated liquid falls. Both exit at the bottom.

🔑 The Falling Film Evaporator is the preferred evaporator for heat-sensitive pharmaceutical products (fruit juices, vitamins, proteins) because residence time is only 5–30 seconds and liquid temperature stays close to boiling point under vacuum — minimising thermal degradation.

Principle

Feed enters the top tube sheet and is distributed evenly into vertical tubes (typically 25–75 mm diameter, 3–8 m long). The liquid wets the tube wall and flows downward as a thin falling film under gravity. Steam (or hot water) condenses on the outside of the tubes — supplying latent heat to the falling film — causing rapid evaporation. Vapour generated rises up through the tube centre counter-current to liquid flow.

Advantages

  • ✅ Very short residence time (seconds) — minimal thermal degradation
  • ✅ Low temperature difference needed (as little as 3°C ΔT between steam and boiling liquid)
  • ✅ Low pressure drop — suitable for vacuum operation → lower boiling point → less heat damage
  • ✅ Continuous operation — high throughput
  • ✅ Can be used as multi-effect evaporator — vapour from first effect heats second effect
  • ❌ Requires even liquid distribution — poor distribution → dry spots → fouling
  • ❌ Not suitable for high-viscosity liquids or crystallising solutions
  • ❌ Minimum wetting rate required — cannot operate at very low feed rates

Pharmaceutical Applications

  • Concentration of antibiotic fermentation broths (penicillin, erythromycin)
  • Concentration of vitamin solutions (ascorbic acid, B-vitamins)
  • Concentration of fruit extracts and herbal extracts for syrup manufacture
  • Evaporation of solvent from API solutions in chemical synthesis
  • Production of WFI (Water for Injection) — falling film multi-effect still
🔑 WFI production: multi-effect falling film evaporator (3–7 effects) evaporates and re-condenses water. Each successive effect operates at lower pressure (lower boiling point). The vapour from the last effect is condensed as WFI. Energy efficiency: 7-effect still uses 1 kg steam to produce 7 kg WFI.
Q1. The Falling Film Evaporator is preferred for heat-sensitive pharmaceuticals because:
✅ Thermal protection in falling film evaporator: (1) Short residence time — liquid passes through tubes in 5–30 seconds; (2) Vacuum operation — reduced pressure lowers boiling point (e.g., at 50 mbar, water boils at ~33°C instead of 100°C); (3) Thin film — very rapid heat transfer, minimal temperature gradient through liquid. Compare with short-tube (calandria) evaporator: liquid recirculates for minutes. Falling film avoids recirculation = minimal thermal exposure = suitable for vitamins, enzymes, antibiotics.
Q2. In a multi-effect falling film evaporator for WFI production, the vapour from the first effect is used to:
✅ Multiple effect evaporation energy saving: each kg of steam evaporates approximately 1 kg water in single effect. In 7-effect still: same 1 kg steam evaporates ~6.5 kg water (some loss to heat losses). Mechanism: Steam → Effect 1 (at P1, T1) → evaporates water → water vapour at T1 → condenses in Effect 2 (at P2 < P1, T2 < T1) → heats Effect 2 → more evaporation → continues. Each successive effect at lower pressure → lower boiling point. Final condensate = WFI. IP/USP specification: WFI conductivity ≤ 1.3 µS/cm at 25°C.
Q3. Which type of pharmaceutical product is NOT suitable for concentration in a Falling Film Evaporator?
✅ Falling film limitations for crystallising solutions: as NaCl concentration approaches saturation during evaporation, crystals nucleate and deposit on the hot tube walls → fouling → tube blockage → uneven film distribution → more fouling → eventually tubes block completely. Alternative: forced circulation evaporator (solution pumped at high velocity — crystals kept in suspension) or crystallising evaporator (Oslo type). Falling film best for: clear solutions, non-viscous liquids (η < 200 mPa·s), non-scaling products, where short residence time is critical.
