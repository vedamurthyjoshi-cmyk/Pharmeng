What you are seeing

A thin tube with one opening facing the flow direction (stagnation point - measures total pressure) and another opening perpendicular to flow (measures static pressure only). The difference equals the dynamic pressure, giving local fluid velocity. Used for point velocity measurement and velocity profiles.

Key Point: Pitot tube equation: v = square root of (2 times deltaP divided by rho) where deltaP = total pressure minus static pressure = dynamic pressure = half-rho-v-squared. Pharmaceutical applications: measuring airflow velocity in HVAC ducts and cleanrooms (ensures adequate air changes per hour); mapping velocity profiles in pipes during validation.
