What you are seeing

A bent tube points UPSTREAM into the flow (stagnation point). The flow is brought to rest at the tube tip -- all kinetic energy converts to pressure (total pressure P_total = P_static + 0.5*rho*v^2). A second port at the side (perpendicular to flow) measures only static pressure P_static. The difference = dynamic pressure = 0.5*rho*v^2. From this, local velocity v = sqrt(2*deltaP/rho).

Key Point: The Pitot tube measures LOCAL VELOCITY at one point in the pipe -- not average flow. For average flow, must traverse (move tube across the pipe cross-section) and average multiple readings. Used extensively in pharmaceutical HVAC for measuring airflow velocity in ducts and cleanrooms.

Pitot Tube -- Theory

  • Stagnation point: Tip of forward-facing tube; all kinetic energy converts to pressure; P_total measured
  • Static port: Perpendicular holes in the outer tube; measures undisturbed static pressure P_static
  • Differential pressure: deltaP = P_total - P_static = 0.5*rho*v^2; gives local velocity
  • Traversing: Move Pitot across pipe at multiple radial positions; average gives mean velocity; mean velocity x area = volumetric flow Q
  • Pharmaceutical use: Measuring unidirectional airflow velocity in Grade A laminar flow hoods (must be 0.45 +/- 0.02 m/s); HVAC duct velocity measurement; air change rate verification in cleanrooms

Q1. The Pitot tube measures velocity by bringing the flow to a complete stop at the tube tip because:
Correct. Bernoulli at stagnation point: P_static + 0.5*rho*v^2 = P_total + 0 (v=0 at stagnation). Therefore P_total - P_static = 0.5*rho*v^2. Rearranging: v = sqrt(2*deltaP/rho). For air at 20C (rho=1.2 kg/m^3): v = sqrt(2*deltaP/1.2). If deltaP = 12 Pa: v = sqrt(20) = 4.47 m/s -- typical clean room supply air velocity.
Q2. In pharmaceutical Grade A cleanroom validation, the Pitot tube is used to verify:
Correct. ISO 14644-3 cleanroom testing: Pitot tube traversal at the HEPA filter face and at the critical zone (open product level). Acceptance criteria: 0.45 m/s mean velocity; coefficient of variation less than 20% (ensures uniform flow, no dead spots). Non-uniform Pitot readings indicate HEPA filter damage, blockage, or plenum design issue. Pitot traversal at 4-9 points across the filter face gives the spatial velocity profile.
