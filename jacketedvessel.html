What you are seeing

The inner vessel contains pharmaceutical product (being heated or cooled). Heating/cooling fluid (steam, hot water, or brine) circulates through the JACKET (space between inner vessel and outer shell). An AGITATOR inside mixes the product continuously -- improving heat transfer to the vessel wall and ensuring uniform temperature throughout the batch.

Key Point: The Jacketed Vessel is the most common heating/cooling equipment in pharmaceutical batch manufacturing. Indirect heat transfer through the vessel wall keeps the heating/cooling medium completely separate from the product -- essential for contamination prevention.

Q1. The agitator in a jacketed vessel improves heat transfer because:
Correct. Stagnant boundary layer concept: without agitation, a layer of liquid adjacent to the vessel wall reaches thermal equilibrium with the wall -- no further heat transfer to bulk liquid. Agitator continuously replaces this stagnant layer with fresh bulk liquid -- maintaining high temperature gradient -- rapid, uniform heating/cooling. Agitator type: anchor agitator for viscous products; pitched blade or Rushton for low-viscosity liquids.
Q2. Half-pipe jacket design is preferred over conventional jacket in pharmaceutical manufacturing because:
Correct. Conventional jacket: annular space around vessel -- jacket fluid can bypass without contacting all surfaces (channelling) -- uneven heating. Half-pipe jacket: semicircular pipes welded in a helical spiral around the vessel exterior -- jacket fluid forced to flow in one path along the entire vessel surface -- no channelling -- uniform heat distribution. Also: higher pressure rating (reduces steam condensate problems) and each weld is accessible for inspection (GMP requirement for pressure vessel certification).
