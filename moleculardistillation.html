What you are seeing

Heated rotor (left, orange) evaporates liquid film in very high vacuum. Molecules travel directly across the short gap (2-3 cm) to the cold condenser (right, blue) without collisions — no equilibrium, no reflux. Heavier molecules (larger) travel slower and hit the condenser at different positions. Both distillate (lighter) and residue (heavier) collected separately.

Key Point: In molecular distillation, the mean free path of molecules (distance between collisions) exceeds the evaporator-condenser distance. Molecules travel ballistically — no vapour-liquid equilibrium. Operates at 0.001-0.01 mbar. Product temperature stays below 100 degrees C despite boiling points of 200-300 degrees C.

Q1. Molecular distillation differs from conventional distillation because:
Correct. Mean free path (lambda) at 0.001 mbar = approximately 50-100 mm. Evaporator-condenser gap = 20-50 mm. Since lambda > gap: molecules evaporate and travel to condenser without hitting other molecules. No equilibrium means no reflux, no theoretical plates — separation is by molecular weight and evaporation rate difference. Heavier molecules (higher MW, lower vapour pressure) are less likely to reach the condenser = separation by molecular weight. Enables gentle distillation of vitamins A, D, E, fatty acids, monoglycerides.
Q2. Molecular distillation is the method of choice for purifying Vitamin E (tocopherol, bp 200-220 degrees C under vacuum):
Correct. Vitamin E distillation: conventional vacuum distillation at 1 mbar: bp approximately 180 degrees C. At 0.001 mbar (molecular distillation): bp effectively 120-140 degrees C. At 120 degrees C for less than 1 minute residence time: Vitamin E stable. At 180 degrees C for extended time: oxidative degradation, colour formation, loss of bioactivity. Molecular distillation preserves bioactivity of heat-sensitive vitamins, omega-3 fatty acids, high-value natural extracts. Also used for: fish oil concentration, monoglyceride purification, lanolin fractionation.
