What you are seeing

The spinning bowl contains a stack of conical discs with narrow gaps between them. Feed enters at the bottom. Centrifugal force separates heavy phase (to outer wall) and light phase (up through disc stack to central outlet). Solids collect at bowl periphery and are discharged through nozzles or intermittently through ports.

The Disc Centrifuge is the standard continuous centrifuge for liquid-liquid separation (O/W emulsions, cream from milk) and liquid clarification in pharmaceutical manufacturing. The disc stack dramatically increases the effective settling area.

Q1. The disc stack in a Disc Centrifuge improves separation efficiency because:
✅ Sigma theory for disc centrifuge: equivalent settling area Σ = (2πn × ω² × (r₃³-r₂³)) / (3g × tan θ) where n = number of disc intervals, r₂,r₃ = inner/outer disc radii, θ = disc half-angle. Increasing n (more discs) or decreasing gap proportionally increases Σ and thus clarification capacity. Pharmaceutical application: clarification of cell culture broth (biopharmaceuticals) — continuous clarification at 10,000–50,000 L/hr with automatic solids discharge.
Q2. A self-cleaning (hermetic) disc centrifuge automatically discharges solids by:
✅ Self-cleaning mechanism: hydraulic system opens peripheral ports (2–4 per bowl) for 0.1–0.3 seconds every few minutes → centrifugal force ejects accumulated solids through open ports into a collecting trough → ports close → operation continues. This allows true continuous operation even with solids-containing feeds. Critical for pharmaceutical fermentation broth clarification where cells must be continuously removed from the product-containing supernatant.
