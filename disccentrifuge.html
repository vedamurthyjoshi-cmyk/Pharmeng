What you are seeing

The spinning bowl contains a stack of conical discs with narrow gaps (~0.5mm) between them. Feed enters at the bottom centre. Centrifugal force separates heavy phase (to outer wall) from light phase (up through disc stack to central outlet). The corrugated disc stack multiplies the effective settling area by 100x compared to an empty bowl. Solids collect at the bowl periphery and are ejected through nozzles or intermittent ports.

Key Point: The disc stack dramatically increases separation efficiency -- each thin disc gap acts as an independent shallow settling chamber. The particle only needs to travel the short disc gap distance (0.5mm) to settle, not the full bowl radius.

Disc Centrifuge -- Design Features

  • Disc stack: 50-200 conical discs; disc angle 35-50 degrees; gap 0.5-1mm between discs
  • Speed: 4,000-10,000 rpm generating 4,000-15,000xg
  • Continuous operation: Feed in, light phase out, solids either discharged through nozzles (nozzle bowl) or periodic automatic discharge (self-cleaning bowl)
  • Clarification rate: 10,000-50,000 L/hr -- far higher than tubular bowl
  • Pharmaceutical use: Clarification of fermentation broth (biopharmaceuticals); continuous cream separation; liquid-liquid extraction in continuous mode
Key Fact: Self-cleaning disc centrifuge opens peripheral ports for 0.1-0.3 seconds every few minutes to eject accumulated solids by centrifugal force -- then ports close and operation continues. This allows true continuous operation even with solids-containing feeds.
Q1. The disc stack in a Disc Centrifuge improves separation efficiency because:
Correct. Sigma theory: equivalent settling area = (2*pi*n * omega^2 * (r3^3-r2^3)) / (3*g*tan(theta)) where n = number of disc intervals. More discs = proportionally more settling area = higher clarification capacity at the same centrifugal force.
Q2. For continuous clarification of pharmaceutical fermentation broth, the Disc Centrifuge is preferred over the Tubular Bowl because:
Correct. Biopharmaceutical manufacturing: monoclonal antibody fermentation produces 1,000-10,000 litres of broth. After fermentation ends, cells must be removed continuously and rapidly (product degrades at room temperature). Self-cleaning disc centrifuge clarifies 10,000-50,000 L/hr continuously -- the only practical option for this scale.
