🔨 Hammer Mill — Impact Grinding

Pharmaceutical Engineering BP304T · Chapter 1

💡 What you are seeing

The central rotor spins at high speed (2000–8000 rpm). Swinging hammers strike incoming feed particles by impact. Broken particles hit the breaker plates (walls) for further size reduction by attrition. Particles only exit through the screen when small enough.

🔑 Size control: The screen aperture size determines the final particle size. Smaller screen = finer product. Typical range: 50–1000 µm.

Principle

The Hammer Mill reduces particle size primarily by impact. A high-speed rotating disc (rotor) carries a series of hammers (rectangular metal bars). Feed material enters from the top, is struck by hammers and broken. Particles bounce off breaker plates (further attrition) and circulate until small enough to pass through a screen at the bottom.

Construction

  • Rotor: Central shaft with disc; rotates at 2000–8000 rpm driven by electric motor
  • Hammers: Rectangular steel bars, pivoted or fixed to rotor disc; 4–16 hammers per mill; swing freely (swing-hammer type) or fixed (rigid)
  • Breaker plates: Corrugated steel plates lining the mill casing; particles bounce off these for additional size reduction by attrition
  • Screen: Perforated metal screen at the bottom; particles only exit when diameter less than screen aperture
  • Feed hopper: Top inlet for material feed; gravity-fed or force-fed

Pharmaceutical Applications

  • Granulation — breaking dried granules to the correct size before tablet compression
  • Milling of herbs and plant material
  • Production of powders from compacted solids
  • Breaking of agglomerates in dried spray-dried powders
🔑 The Hammer Mill is the most widely used milling equipment in pharmaceutical tablet manufacturing — particularly for granule sizing after wet or dry granulation.

Advantages and Disadvantages

  • ✅ High throughput — fast continuous operation
  • ✅ Easy to change particle size — just change screen
  • ✅ Easy to clean and maintain
  • ✅ Handles wide variety of materials — hard, soft, fibrous
  • ❌ Generates heat — not suitable for heat-sensitive drugs
  • ❌ Creates dust — needs dust collection system
  • ❌ Screen may blind (clog) with sticky or moist material
  • ❌ Wide particle size distribution — not for very narrow specifications
Q1. The primary mechanism of size reduction in a Hammer Mill is:
✅ Hammer Mill = Impact + Attrition. Impact: hammers strike particles at 2000–8000 rpm → high kinetic energy → fracture. Attrition: broken particles bounce off corrugated breaker plates → further size reduction by rubbing. Compare with Ball Mill (also impact + attrition but slower; cascade mechanism).
Q2. Particle size from a Hammer Mill is controlled by:
✅ Screen (sieve) at bottom of hammer mill is the size-control mechanism. Material recirculates inside mill until particles are small enough to pass through screen apertures. Changing screen size (50 µm to 5 mm) changes product particle size. This makes the hammer mill very flexible for different particle size specifications.
Q3. In pharmaceutical tablet manufacturing, the Hammer Mill is most commonly used for:
✅ Granule sizing: after wet granulation and drying (in fluid bed dryer or tray oven), dried granules are passed through a Hammer Mill to break down oversized granules to uniform size (typically 250–850 µm for tablets). Uniform granule size = uniform die filling = consistent tablet weight = good content uniformity.
Q4. The Hammer Mill is NOT suitable for which type of material?
✅ Heat generation in hammer mill: at 5000+ rpm, hammers generate significant friction and impact heat. Temperature rise of 20–40°C possible for difficult materials. Thermolabile APIs (proteins, enzymes, some vitamins) would denature. Solution: cryogenic milling (liquid nitrogen cooling) or use fluid energy mill (compressed gas — minimal heat).
