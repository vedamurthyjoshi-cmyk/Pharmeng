What you are seeing

Feed liquid enters at the BOTTOM of long vertical tubes. Steam condenses on the OUTSIDE of the tubes, heating the liquid. As the liquid begins to boil, vapour bubbles form inside the tubes. The rising vapour bubbles drag the liquid upward as a thin climbing film. Concentrated liquid and vapour exit together at the TOP. Residence time: 1-5 minutes.

Key Point: Climbing Film Evaporator is the OPPOSITE of the Falling Film -- liquid is pushed UP by vapour momentum rather than falling by gravity. This makes it suitable for slightly more viscous feeds but gives longer residence time than Falling Film.

Q1. In the Climbing Film Evaporator, liquid rises upward because:
Correct. Two-phase flow dynamics: as liquid boils near the tube wall, vapour bubbles nucleate and rise rapidly. The buoyancy and momentum of rising vapour bubbles entrains surrounding liquid -- lifting the liquid film upward against gravity. This mechanism gives the climbing film its characteristic upward co-current vapour-liquid flow.
Q2. The Climbing Film Evaporator is LESS suitable than Falling Film for heat-sensitive products because:
Correct. Residence time comparison: Falling Film -- liquid falls quickly by gravity, exits in 5-30 seconds -- minimal thermal exposure. Climbing Film -- liquid rises slowly driven by vapour momentum, takes 1-5 minutes to traverse the tube. For vitamins, enzymes, antibiotics -- the shorter the heat exposure the better -- Falling Film is preferred.
