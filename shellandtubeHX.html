What you are seeing

Cold fluid (blue) flows INSIDE the horizontal tubes at high velocity. Hot fluid (red) enters the shell at one end and flows ACROSS and AROUND the outside of the tubes, directed by baffles. Heat transfers through the tube walls. In counter-current flow (shown), fluids flow in opposite directions -- maximising the temperature difference (LMTD) at every point along the exchanger.

Key Point: The Shell-and-Tube HX is the most common heat exchanger in pharmaceutical manufacturing. Counter-current flow gives higher LMTD (Log Mean Temperature Difference) than co-current -- more heat transferred per unit area -- more efficient.

Q1. In the Shell-and-Tube Heat Exchanger, baffles are installed on the shell side to:
Correct. Without baffles, shell-side fluid would flow straight through with minimal contact with tubes -- very poor heat transfer. Baffles force fluid to flow perpendicular to tubes (crossflow) -- this gives turbulent flow (high Re) even at moderate velocities -- high shell-side heat transfer coefficient (h_s). Typical baffle cut: 25% -- allowing 25% of shell cross-section as flow passage.
Q2. Pharmaceutical-grade shell-and-tube heat exchangers for WFI cooling use which design feature:
Correct. GMP sanitary HX requirements for WFI/pharmaceutical service: (1) All wetted parts SS 316L or better; (2) Ra surface finish less than 0.8 micrometres (electropolished); (3) No dead legs (any stagnant water volume breeds bacteria); (4) Full CIP (clean-in-place) and SIP (steam-in-place) capability; (5) Drainable design -- no trapped liquid; (6) Double tube sheets for critical applications -- if tube develops a leak, product and shell fluid cannot mix undetected.
