What you are seeing

A flat plate with a sharp-edged circular hole (orifice) is inserted perpendicular to flow. Fluid accelerates through the orifice. Pressure drops sharply across the plate. The flow stream contracts to a minimum cross-section (VENA CONTRACTA) just downstream. Pressure measured at the upstream tap (P1) and at the vena contracta (P2). DeltaP related to flow rate by Bernoulli.

Key Point: The Orifice Meter is simpler and cheaper than the Venturimeter but has much higher permanent pressure loss (60% of deltaP lost) because the sudden expansion downstream creates turbulence. Discharge coefficient Cd approximately 0.6 (vs 0.98 for Venturimeter).

Orifice Meter -- Theory

  • Orifice plate: Flat SS plate; orifice diameter = 0.5-0.8 times pipe diameter; sharp upstream edge; bevelled downstream edge
  • Pressure taps: Corner taps (most common), flange taps, or D-D/2 taps
  • Vena contracta: Point of minimum stream cross-section just downstream of orifice; slightly smaller than orifice area
  • Cd approximately 0.6: Accounts for vena contracta contraction (Cc) and velocity coefficient (Cv)
  • Pharmaceutical use: Steam flow, compressed air, WFI flow measurement in utilities
Key Point: The Orifice Meter is simpler and cheaper than the Venturimeter but has much higher permanent pressure loss (60% of deltaP lost) because the sudden expansion downstream creates turbulence. Discharge coefficient Cd approximately 0.6 (vs 0.98 for Venturimeter).
Q1. The discharge coefficient of an Orifice Meter (Cd approximately 0.6) is much lower than a Venturimeter (Cd approximately 0.98) because:
Correct. Cd = Cc x Cv where Cc = contraction coefficient (vena contracta area / orifice area, approximately 0.64) and Cv = velocity coefficient (accounts for friction losses, approximately 0.97). Venturimeter: smooth contraction and gradual expansion -- no vena contracta, no turbulent expansion -- Cc approximately 1.0, Cv approximately 0.98, so Cd approximately 0.98. Orifice: Cd approximately 0.64 x 0.97 approximately 0.62.
Q2. In pharmaceutical GMP utilities, the Orifice Meter is used for steam flow measurement rather than Venturimeter because:
Correct. Cost vs performance trade-off: Venturimeter costs 5-10 times more than an equivalent orifice plate and requires a longer straight pipe run. For steam, compressed air, nitrogen, and cooling water (utility services) -- the pressure loss is acceptable because these lines operate at higher pressure with more available head. Product-contact WFI lines: Venturimeter preferred because lower pressure loss maintains WFI loop pressure and circulation velocity, preventing bacterial growth.
