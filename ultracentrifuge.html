What you are seeing

The rotor spins at 40,000–100,000 rpm inside a vacuum chamber. Centrifugal forces of 100,000–600,000×g sediment macromolecules like proteins, DNA, and viruses at rates determined by their sedimentation coefficient (Svedberg unit). Used for molecular weight determination and separation of biologics.

The ultracentrifuge operates in high vacuum to eliminate air friction on the fast-spinning rotor. Without vacuum, air resistance would generate enormous heat and the rotor would fail. The vacuum also prevents sample evaporation.

Q1. The sedimentation coefficient (S, Svedberg unit) measured in an ultracentrifuge is defined as:
✅ S = v/(ω²r) is a physical property of the macromolecule (at a given temperature and solvent). From S, molecular weight can be calculated: M = (S × R × T) / (D × (1 − v̄ρ)) [Svedberg equation] where D = diffusion coefficient, v̄ = partial specific volume, ρ = solvent density. Pharmaceutical use: QC of viral vaccines (verify particle sedimentation matches expected S value = correct assembly); mAb aggregation detection (monomer ~7S vs aggregate ~14S); PEGylated protein characterisation.
Q2. Ultracentrifugation is used in pharmaceutical manufacture primarily for:
✅ Viral vaccine purification by density gradient ultracentrifugation: (1) Crude harvest layered on sucrose or CsCl gradient; (2) Centrifuge at 100,000×g for 2–4 hours; (3) Virus band forms at characteristic buoyant density (e.g., influenza: 1.20 g/mL in sucrose); (4) Band collected by tube puncture or fractionation; (5) Product: highly purified virus at defined density — proven free from cellular DNA and protein contaminants. Used for: polio vaccine (Salk); influenza; rabies; hepatitis B VLP; COVID-19 VLP vaccine candidates.
