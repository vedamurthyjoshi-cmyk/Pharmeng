What you are seeing

The rotor spins at 40,000-100,000 rpm inside a VACUUM chamber (to eliminate air friction). Centrifugal forces of 100,000-600,000xg sediment macromolecules at rates determined by their Svedberg (S) values. In density gradient mode, a sucrose or CsCl gradient separates particles by buoyant density. Each type of particle (virus, ribosome, protein) forms a distinct band at its characteristic density.

Key Point: Ultracentrifugation operates in high vacuum because at 100,000 rpm, air friction would generate enough heat to destroy both the rotor and the sample within seconds. The vacuum also prevents sample evaporation.

Ultracentrifuge -- Svedberg Units and Applications

  • Speed: 40,000-100,000 rpm; force 100,000-600,000xg
  • Vacuum: Less than 1 microbar -- eliminates air friction completely
  • Svedberg unit (S): 1S = 10^-13 seconds; S = velocity / (omega^2 * r) -- characterises sedimentation rate per unit centrifugal force
  • Examples: Albumin 4.5S; IgG 7S; ribosome 70S (prokaryote) 80S (eukaryote); influenza virus 600-900S
  • Pharmaceutical use: Viral vaccine purification (density gradient); protein characterisation; nanoparticle characterisation; DNA/RNA isolation
Key Fact: Density gradient ultracentrifugation for vaccine purification -- virus (e.g., influenza, poliovirus) bands at its characteristic buoyant density (1.20-1.25 g/mL in sucrose gradient) -- separated from cell debris (lighter) and DNA/protein impurities -- produces highly pure virus preparation.
Q1. The ultracentrifuge must operate in high vacuum because:
Correct. At 60,000rpm, rotor surface velocity exceeds 500 m/s. Air friction at this speed generates enormous heat (similar to atmospheric re-entry heating). Vacuum reduces air pressure to less than 1 microbar -- essentially zero air molecules -- zero friction -- rotor and sample remain at safe temperature. Without vacuum, rotor would reach destructive temperatures within minutes.
Q2. In density gradient ultracentrifugation for vaccine purification, the virus forms a distinct band because:
Correct. Isopycnic (equilibrium) density gradient: particles sediment until they reach the point where their buoyant density equals the local gradient density -- then they stop -- forming a sharp band. Influenza virus: bands at 1.20 g/mL in sucrose. Cell membranes: 1.14-1.18 g/mL. DNA: 1.70 g/mL (in CsCl). Each component at its own characteristic density -- excellent separation even from complex biological mixtures.
