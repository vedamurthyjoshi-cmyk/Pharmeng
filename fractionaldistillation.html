What you are seeing

Vapour (yellow) rises up the fractionating column — meeting descending condensate (blue) at each theoretical plate. Each meeting creates a vapour-liquid equilibrium. More volatile component concentrates in rising vapour. Gradient bar shows temperature decreasing from hot (bottom) to cool (top). Pure component exits at top.

Key Point: The more theoretical plates in the column, the better the separation of closely-boiling components. Used when boiling point difference is less than 25 degrees C.

Principle

A fractionating column provides multiple vapour-liquid equilibrium stages between the flask and condenser. Rising vapour meets descending condensate — at each theoretical plate, volatile component transfers from liquid to vapour phase and less-volatile component transfers from vapour to liquid. After many plates, pure volatile component exits at top; pure less-volatile component remains at bottom.

Pharmaceutical Applications

  • Purification of ethanol for pharmaceutical use (spirits of wine, rectified spirit)
  • Recovery of mixed solvents from pharmaceutical synthesis — separating ethanol/water, acetone/water, IPA/water
  • Purification of glycerol (by-product of soap manufacture) for pharmaceutical use
  • Separation of essential oil components (terpenoids) for fragrance and flavour
Q1. The number of theoretical plates (NTP) in a fractionating column determines:
NTP and separation: each theoretical plate provides one vapour-liquid equilibrium stage. At each stage, the vapour becomes richer in the more volatile component by a factor related to the relative volatility (alpha = P_A/P_B, ratio of vapour pressures). More plates = more enrichment steps = purer distillate for same feed composition. McCabe-Thiele graphical method: step between operating line and equilibrium curve on y-x diagram counts NTP. High-performance columns: >100 NTP possible (packed columns, efficiency plates).
Q2. In pharmaceutical solvent recovery, fractional distillation is preferred over simple distillation for recovering ethanol from aqueous solutions because:
Ethanol-water azeotrope: at 95.6% ethanol by weight (89.4 mol%), the vapour and liquid compositions are identical — this is the azeotropic composition. Fractional distillation cannot separate beyond the azeotrope regardless of number of plates. The distillate approaches 95.6% ethanol maximum. For absolute ethanol (99.9%+): need to break the azeotrope by (1) adding benzene or cyclohexane (azeotropic distillation — breaks azeotrope but toxic solvent); (2) molecular sieves (adsorb water selectively); (3) pervaporation membranes. IP definition: Rectified Spirit = 95% v/v ethanol (near azeotrope).
