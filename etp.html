Effluent Treatment Plant (ETP)

ETPs treat pharmaceutical wastewater before discharge to meet CPCB/pollution control norms. Pharma effluents contain solvents, APIs, detergents, and high BOD/COD loads.

Stage 1 — Primary Treatment

Screening removes large solids. Equalisation tank homogenises flow and pH. Oil and grease trap removes hydrocarbons. pH adjustment to 6.5–8.5.

Stage 2 — Secondary Treatment (Biological)

Activated sludge process (ASP) or Moving Bed Biofilm Reactor (MBBR) uses microorganisms to degrade organic matter. Aeration tank with diffusers supplies oxygen. Secondary clarifier settles biomass (sludge). 80–90% BOD removal achieved.

Stage 3 — Tertiary Treatment

Sand/multimedia filtration, activated carbon adsorption to remove colour and residual APIs. UV/ozone disinfection kills pathogens. Reverse osmosis for zero liquid discharge (ZLD) compliance.

Sludge Handling

Sludge from clarifiers is thickened, dewatered in filter press, and disposed as hazardous waste per CPCB norms.

Regulatory Compliance

Output must meet: BOD < 30 mg/L, COD < 250 mg/L, TSS < 100 mg/L, pH 6.5–8.5 before discharge.
