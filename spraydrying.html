💡 What you are seeing

Liquid feed is atomised into tiny droplets from the nozzle at the top. Droplets contact hot air (150–250°C inlet). Solvent evaporates in milliseconds — the droplet shrinks and dries. Blue = liquid droplet, orange = partially dried, dark orange = dry powder. Dry particles collect at the bottom; fine particles go to the cyclone separator.

🔑 Despite the inlet air temperature of 150–250°C, the product stays cool (60–80°C) because evaporating solvent absorbs the heat energy — this is the Joule-Thomson / wet-bulb principle. This is why spray drying is suitable for many heat-sensitive APIs.

Principle

A liquid feed (solution, suspension, or emulsion) is pumped through an atomiser (nozzle or spinning disc) into a hot air stream (150–250°C) inside a drying chamber. Each droplet (30–300 µm diameter) has enormous surface area — solvent evaporates in less than 0.1 second. The dry solid particle falls to the chamber base; fine particles are carried to the cyclone separator by exhaust air.

Types of Atomisers

  • Two-fluid nozzle: Liquid jet shattered by compressed gas → fine droplets (10–150 µm); most common in pharmaceutical spray drying
  • Rotary atomiser (spinning disc): Liquid fed onto spinning disc (10,000–30,000 rpm) → thrown off by centrifugal force → fine droplets; high throughput; large chambers needed
  • Pressure nozzle (single fluid): High-pressure liquid forced through small orifice; simple but narrow size range

Pharmaceutical Applications

  • Amorphous Solid Dispersions (ASD): Drug + polymer dissolved in solvent → spray dried → molecular dispersion in polymer matrix → improved solubility. Commercial: Sporanox (itraconazole), Kaletra (lopinavir/ritonavir)
  • Inhalation dry powder (DPI): Spray drying produces spherical particles with controlled aerodynamic diameter (1–5 µm) for lung deposition
  • Microencapsulation: Drug droplets coated with polymer shell during spray drying
  • Infant formula, food powders: Large-scale spray drying of dairy products
🔑 ASD by spray drying: rapid solvent evaporation (< 0.1 s) kinetically traps drug in amorphous state in polymer — insufficient time for crystal nucleation. HPMC-AS or copovidone as carrier polymer. Zelboraf (vemurafenib): 37× AUC improvement with ASD vs crystalline formulation.
Q1. Spray drying is used to manufacture Amorphous Solid Dispersions (ASD) because:
✅ ASD formation kinetics: for crystallisation to occur, drug molecules need time to find each other, nucleate, and grow crystals. Spray drying: droplet lifetime = 0.05–0.1 seconds → solvent evaporation rate >> crystal nucleation rate → drug molecules are immobilised in the polymer glass before they can crystallise → kinetically trapped amorphous dispersion. Key parameters: inlet/outlet temperature (controls evaporation rate), concentration ratio (drug:polymer), and polymer type (HPMC-AS, copovidone provide glass-forming matrix).
Q2. Despite inlet air temperature of 150–250°C in spray drying, the product temperature remains 60–80°C because:
✅ Wet-bulb cooling in spray drying: as long as solvent is evaporating from droplet → latent heat of evaporation (2.4 kJ/g for water; 0.85 kJ/g for ethanol) absorbs energy from hot air → droplet temperature stays near wet-bulb temperature. For ethanol solvent: wet-bulb T ≈ 40–60°C even with 150°C inlet air. Only after all solvent has evaporated does particle temperature rise toward outlet air temperature (~70–80°C). This thermal protection allows spray drying of temperature-sensitive APIs that would denature at 150°C if exposed directly.
Q3. In a pharmaceutical spray dryer, the cyclone separator is positioned:
✅ Spray dryer powder collection: two streams — (1) Main powder: larger/heavier dried particles settle to conical chamber base by gravity → discharged via rotary valve at bottom. (2) Fine powder: particles < 20 µm carried upward by exhaust air → cyclone separator → centrifugal force separates fines from air → collected in cyclone receiver. Both streams combined = total product. Cyclone efficiency for spray-dried particles (10–100 µm): 95–99%. Bag filter downstream polishes remaining very fine particles (< 5 µm) from exhaust before air discharged.
