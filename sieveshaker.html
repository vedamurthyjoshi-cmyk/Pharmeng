💡 What you are seeing

A stack of sieves vibrates horizontally. Particles too large to pass through a sieve are retained on it (shown as larger coloured circles staying on their sieve). Smaller particles fall through to the next sieve. The smallest particles reach the receiver pan at the bottom. After shaking, each sieve is weighed to determine particle size distribution.

🔑 Sieve arrangement: LARGEST aperture at TOP (2000 µm) → progressively smaller → SMALLEST at BOTTOM (63 µm) → Receiver pan. Particles are retained on the sieve with aperture just smaller than their size.

Principle

Sieve analysis separates a powder sample into fractions by particle size using a stack of sieves with progressively smaller apertures. The stack vibrates (mechanical shaker) for 5–20 minutes — particles smaller than each sieve aperture pass through; larger particles are retained. The mass retained on each sieve is weighed and expressed as % of total — giving the Particle Size Distribution (PSD).

Standard Sieve Sizes (IP / BSS)

  • Sieve No. 8 — 2360 µm | Sieve No. 10 — 2000 µm | Sieve No. 22 — 710 µm
  • Sieve No. 44 — 355 µm | Sieve No. 60 — 250 µm | Sieve No. 85 — 180 µm
  • Sieve No. 120 — 125 µm | Sieve No. 170 — 90 µm | Sieve No. 200 — 75 µm
  • Sieve No. 240 — 63 µm
🔑 IP sieve numbering: the number approximates the number of openings per inch. Higher number = finer sieve = smaller aperture. BSS (British Standard Sieve) numbers used in IP/BP.

Pharmaceutical Applications

  • Quality control of granules before tablet compression — IP specification: granule size 250–850 µm
  • Particle size analysis of API powders for dissolution testing
  • Classification of powders for IP descriptors — coarse (≥ 1400 µm), moderately coarse (≥ 355 µm), moderately fine (≥ 180 µm), fine (≥ 125 µm), very fine (≥ 45 µm)
  • Monitoring granulation endpoint — sieve analysis of dried granules confirms correct PSD
Q1. In a sieve stack for particle size analysis, sieves are arranged with the largest aperture at the top because:
✅ Top-to-bottom: coarsest → finest. Example: 2000 µm top sieve retains particles > 2000 µm. Particles 1000–2000 µm pass through first sieve, retained on 1000 µm sieve. And so on down the stack. Pan at bottom collects all particles < smallest sieve aperture. After shaking: each sieve weighed → mass retained → % retained per sieve → PSD expressed as cumulative % passing or retained.
Q2. The IP powder classification "moderately fine powder" means that all particles pass through a sieve with aperture of:
✅ IP powder grades: Very coarse — passes Sieve No. 8 (2360 µm). Coarse — passes No. 10 (2000 µm). Moderately coarse — passes No. 22 (710 µm). Moderately fine — passes No. 85 (180 µm). Fine — passes No. 120 (125 µm). Very fine — passes No. 240 (63 µm). These IP specifications tell pharmacists and manufacturers the required particle size of raw material powders. "All particles pass through" means 100% passes through the specified sieve.
Q3. During sieve analysis of pharmaceutical granules, what does a high % retained on the top sieve (largest aperture) indicate?
✅ Oversized granules (retained on top sieve): indicate granules are too large → may not flow smoothly into die cavity → variable die fill → weight variation → content uniformity failure. Action: pass through hammer mill again (Comil or Frewitt) to break down oversized granules. Target PSD for tablet granules: D90 < 850 µm, D50 = 250–500 µm, < 5% fines below 125 µm.
