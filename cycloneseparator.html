💡 What you are seeing

Dust-laden air enters tangentially at the top of the cone — creating a spinning vortex (outer vortex spirals DOWN). Centrifugal force throws heavy particles outward to the wall — they slide down and collect at the bottom. Clean air forms an inner vortex that spirals UP and exits through the central pipe at the top. No filters — no moving parts.

🔑 Cyclone Separator uses centrifugal force (not a filter) to separate particles from air. Larger/heavier particles (> 10 µm): separated efficiently. Fine particles (< 5 µm): may escape with clean air. No moving parts — very reliable, low maintenance.

Principle

Cyclone separation works by centrifugal force. Particle-laden gas enters tangentially — creating a spinning vortex inside the cone. Centrifugal force (F = mv²/r) throws heavier particles outward to the wall; they lose velocity, fall under gravity, and collect in the hopper at the bottom. The cleaned gas reverses direction (inner vortex) and exits upward through the central vortex finder tube.

Construction

  • Inlet: Rectangular or circular; tangential entry at top of cylindrical section
  • Cylinder section: Upper cylindrical part where tangential entry creates initial vortex
  • Cone section: Tapered lower section; increases rotational velocity as diameter decreases
  • Vortex finder (dip tube): Central tube extending downward from top — clean air exits through this
  • Dust hopper: Collection bin at the very bottom of cone for settled particles

Pharmaceutical Applications

  • Dust collection after milling operations — fluid energy mill, hammer mill exhaust
  • Spray dryer cyclone — main powder collection chamber in spray drying
  • Fluidised bed dryer — bag filters + cyclone for fines collection
  • Pneumatic conveying systems — separating API powder from transport air
🔑 Spray Dryer: cyclone separator is an integral part of every spray dryer — the cyclone collects 95–99% of spray-dried powder from the drying air. The bag filter downstream captures remaining fine particles.
Q1. The driving force for particle separation in a cyclone separator is:
✅ Centrifugal separation: F_centrifugal = mv²/r. Larger particles (higher m) experience greater centrifugal force → thrown more strongly to wall → better collection. Small particles (< 5 µm): centrifugal force insufficient to overcome drag → escape with clean air. Cyclone efficiency typically 85–99% for particles > 10 µm; much lower for < 5 µm (these need bag filter or HEPA). No moving parts → low maintenance, reliable, handles hot gases.
Q2. In a spray dryer, the cyclone separator is positioned:
✅ Spray dryer flow: Feed solution → nozzle atomisation → drying chamber (hot air contact) → dried powder falls to chamber base (main product) + fine powder carried in exhaust air → cyclone separator collects fine powder from exhaust → clean air exits (bag filter for final polishing). Two product streams: (1) chamber base powder (coarser, main product), (2) cyclone powder (finer). Combined for finished product. Cyclone efficiency for spray-dried particles (10–100 µm): typically 95–99%.
