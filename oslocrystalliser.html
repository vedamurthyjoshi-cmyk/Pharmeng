Key principle: Supersaturated solution circulates upward through a bed of growing crystals in the growth chamber. Fine crystals dissolve in the circulator and large crystals grow undisturbed — producing very large, uniform crystals.

The Oslo Crystalliser (also called Krystal or growth-type crystalliser) separates nucleation (in the circulating supersaturated solution) from crystal growth (in the fluidised bed) — key advantage over Swenson-Walker.
Q1. The Oslo Crystalliser produces larger crystals than the Swenson-Walker because:
✅ Oslo design principle: nucleation in circulator (high supersaturation zone) → crystals settle into growth chamber (lower supersaturation) → grow undisturbed. Swenson-Walker: nucleation and growth occur together in same trough — many nuclei compete → more, smaller crystals. Large crystal size advantage: easier filtration; purer product (less surface area for mother liquor retention); better flowability.
Q2. In the Oslo Crystalliser, the supersaturated solution flows:
✅ Fluidised bed principle in Oslo crystalliser: upward liquid velocity = terminal settling velocity of crystals → crystals suspended. Larger crystals settle faster → stay at bottom of bed. Smaller crystals lighter → stay higher. Natural size classification occurs — largest at bottom near discharge. Upward velocity controlled precisely (0.01–0.1 m/s) to match crystal density × size — faster flow → finer crystals carried over → lost.
