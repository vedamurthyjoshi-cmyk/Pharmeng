Watch the colour of each station change as it passes through the four stages: Orange = Filling (granules fall into die), Blue = Pre-compression (light compression removes air), Red = Main compression (full force — tablet formed), Green = Ejection (tablet pushed out).

🔑 The Rotary Tablet Press produces tablets continuously — all four stages happen simultaneously on different stations as the turret rotates. Production rate: 100,000 to 1,000,000+ tablets per hour depending on machine size and speed.

Principle

The Rotary Tablet Press has multiple punch-and-die sets (stations) arranged around the circumference of a rotating turret. As the turret rotates, each station passes through four successive zones: filling → pre-compression → main compression → ejection — continuously, in sequence. All zones are active simultaneously — while one station is being filled, another is being compressed, and another is ejecting a finished tablet.

Four Stages of Tablet Compression

  • Stage 1 — Filling: Lower punch drops to fill position; die cavity fills with granules from feed frame; excess granules scraped off level with die face
  • Stage 2 — Pre-compression (optional): Lower force (5–10% of main force) applied by pre-compression rollers; removes air entrapped between granules; prevents capping
  • Stage 3 — Main compression: Upper and lower punches pass between main compression rollers; full compression force applied (5–100 kN); tablet formed
  • Stage 4 — Ejection: Lower punch rises to ejection cam; pushes tablet up to die face level; tablet deflected by take-off scraper blade into collection chute
🔑 Tablet weight control: the die fill depth (lower punch position during filling) determines granule volume per tablet → tablet weight. Weight adjustment mechanism (WAM) adjusts lower punch fill position. Monitoring: automatic weight control (AWC) uses tablet thickness as proxy for weight (measured in real time).

Critical Quality Attributes (CQAs)

  • Tablet weight: IP spec typically ±5% (average weight < 250 mg) or ±7.5% (> 80 mg); monitored by in-process weight checks every 15 min
  • Tablet hardness: Typically 50–200 N; too low → friable tablets; too high → slow dissolution
  • Tablet thickness: Controlled by main compression roller gap; proxy for weight
  • Disintegration time: IP specification NMT 15 min (uncoated); 60 min (film-coated)
  • Friability: NMT 1.0% (IP) after 100 drum rotations at 25 rpm
Q1. The purpose of pre-compression in a Rotary Tablet Press is:
✅ Air entrapment problem: granules contain inter-particle air voids. When punches compress rapidly, air is trapped. After punch release, compressed air expands elastically → pushes tablet apart → capping (horizontal fracture through tablet) or lamination (tablet splits into layers). Pre-compression (5–10% of main force) at a slow punch speed: squeezes air out of die through small gap between punch and die wall → de-aerated granule bed → main compression gives defect-free tablet. Essential for high-speed tablet presses (> 100,000 tab/hr).
Q2. Tablet weight in a Rotary Tablet Press is primarily controlled by adjusting:
✅ Die fill volume = tablet weight determinant: at constant bulk density, mass = volume × density. Die fill depth controls volume directly. WAM adjustment: lower punch position during filling moved up (shallower) → smaller volume → lighter tablet; moved down (deeper) → larger volume → heavier tablet. Modern automatic weight control (AWC): sensor measures tablet thickness after compression (proxy for weight) → feedback to WAM → automatic correction within each turret revolution. This maintains weight within specification continuously without operator intervention.
Q3. Tablet capping defect (the upper cap of the tablet detaching) is most likely caused by:
✅ Capping causes and solutions: (1) Air entrapment → add pre-compression; reduce compression speed. (2) Brittle granules (too dry, wrong binder) → reformulate; increase binder; adjust LOD. (3) Elastic recovery of over-lubricated granules → reduce MgSt concentration; check mixing time. (4) Excessive fines → screen granulation; adjust particle size distribution. Note: lamination (tablets split into parallel layers) has similar causes but creates horizontal splits throughout the tablet thickness, not just the top cap.
